Your retro games, as real boxes on a shelf.

Drag your games onto the window and they land on the right shelf with their box art, ready to play with a keyboard or a controller. The emulators come inside the app, so there's nothing else to set up.

    The first time

    Opening it the first time

    The app isn't signed by Apple or Microsoft, so your computer asks once whether you trust it. After that it opens normally and updates itself.

    1. Open the .dmg and drag The Retro Shelf into Applications.
    2. Open it from Applications. macOS says it can't check the app: click Done.
    3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll down to Security. Next to "The Retro Shelf was blocked to protect your Mac", click Open Anyway and confirm with your password or Touch ID.
    4. Click Open Anyway once more when asked.
    1. Run the Setup .exe you downloaded.
    2. If a blue "Windows protected your PC" box appears, click More info, then Run anyway.
    3. It installs and opens by itself. For a USB stick, take the portable zip instead: unzip it anywhere and open The Retro Shelf inside. Games, saves and settings stay in its Data folder.
    1. Download the .AppImage and put it somewhere it can stay, like your home folder.
    2. Right-click it, open Properties and allow it to run as a program (or run chmod +x on it).
    3. Double-click it. On a Steam Deck, the Steam Deck guide adds it to Game Mode.

    No games are included. The app's Help → Where to Get Games shows the legal ways to get them.

    Questions

    Good to know

    Where do I get games?

    No games are included, and the app doesn't link to sites that hand out commercial games. You can copy the cartridges and discs you own, play free homebrew, or buy official re-releases. The app's Help → Where to Get Games walks through each of these.

    Some consoles ask for a BIOS file. What's that?

    A small file from inside the console itself, which a few emulators need (Sega CD, Saturn, 3DO, ColecoVision and Intellivision). Copy it from your own console and drop it on the window: it goes in the right place. Settings → Saves & BIOS shows what each console has. Most consoles don't need one, and MSX uses a free one that comes with the app.

    Which controllers work?

    Xbox, PlayStation and Switch Pro controllers, and most others. Settings → While you play → Your controller has a test that lights up every button, and lets you choose your own buttons for each console. Two controllers are numbered player 1 and 2 on screen.

    Does it work on a Steam Deck?

    Yes. Download the Linux AppImage in desktop mode, then add it to Steam with the Steam Deck guide. In Game Mode, TV mode makes everything work with the Deck's controls.

    Is it really free?

    Yes, and open source (GPL-3.0). There's no account, no ads and no tracking, and nothing about your games leaves your computer. Some of the emulators inside are for non-commercial use only, so the app can never be sold.

    Why does my computer warn me about it?

    Apps from Apple and Microsoft's paid developer programs are signed, and this one isn't. Your computer asks once; the steps above get you past it. After that it opens normally, and updates install without asking again.

    How do updates work?

    The app checks in the background and downloads a new version by itself. It's put in place when you restart. To try new features early, turn on Settings → App → Get test versions.

    Something isn't working

    Tell us on GitHub. Settings → App → Copy info puts what helps track it down on your clipboard.
